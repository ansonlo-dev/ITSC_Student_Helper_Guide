# Talking to Users

## The four counter tips

::: tip From the orientation
1. **Listen carefully** to the other person's questions to understand their
   **true situation**.
2. Pay attention to the **speed and tone** of your own speech.
3. Assess the **urgency** of the matter.
4. When seeking help from ITSC colleagues, first clarify whether the enquiry is
   **in person or over the phone**.
:::

Point 4 matters more than it looks. A colleague will drop what they are doing
for someone standing at the counter, but will handle a phone enquiry differently
— so say which it is before you describe the problem.

## Listening for the real question

Users describe the symptom they noticed, not the problem they have. "The printer
is broken" might mean a jam, an empty tray, a closed room, or a job sent to the
wrong printer. Ask what they were trying to do before you accept the diagnosis.

## Speed and tone

- Slow down. You know the system; they do not.
- Match the user's language — English, Cantonese or Mandarin — and switch if
  they seem more comfortable in another.
- Avoid jargon. Say what the user needs to **do**, not what the system is doing.
- Silence makes people anxious. A short update every minute or so is enough.

## Assessing urgency

A class waiting in a room beats a general question at the counter. Ask whether a
class or a deadline is being blocked right now, and act accordingly.

## Saying no

You will often have to decline — account operations in particular are not yours
to perform. Do it clearly and with a next step:

> "That one is beyond what I'm allowed to change, but let me get our duty staff
> to help you right now."

> "I can't reset a password from here, but this self-service page will do it —
> let me walk you through it."

Do not apologise repeatedly, invent a reason, or blame a colleague or "the
system". State the limit, then offer the path forward.

## When a user is upset

1. Let them finish. Do not interrupt or defend.
2. Acknowledge the impact.
3. Restate the problem so they know you heard it.
4. Say what you *can* do right now, and be specific about timing.
5. If they stay angry or want to complain, get a full-time staff member. That is
   the correct action, not a last resort.

Never argue, never raise your voice, and never take it personally. If you feel
unsafe, step away and call [campus security](/reference/contacts).

## Closing the loop

Record the enquiry on the
[Issue Received by ITSC Student Helper](https://forms.office.com/r/30WbQbDCSF)
form, including its status. If you promised a call-back, make sure it happens —
by you or by whoever takes over.
